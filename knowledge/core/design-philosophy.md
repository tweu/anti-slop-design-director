# Design philosophy

The beliefs every decision rests on. These are short on purpose. The other files apply them.

## 1. Design the product, not the trend
Start from what the product does, who uses it and in what conditions. A style is chosen because it
serves that product: a trading desk needs density and calm, a bakery needs appetite and
warmth, a research forum needs reading comfort and findability. If you can't explain a visual
choice in terms of the product, the choice is a habit, not a decision.

*Misuse:* treating "product-first" as "make it plain". Some products need expression to do
their job. Persuade and Experience surfaces earn bold, committed work.

## 2. Mode sets ambition
- **Persuade / Experience:** full commitment. Have a point of view, a memorable first viewport and one
  signature move. Holding back here reads as generic.
- **Operate / Read:** ambition shows up as precision. That means stable structure, familiar patterns,
  scannable density and careful detail. Brand appears in small, exact choices, not spectacle.
(Impeccable modes; UIZZE persuade/operate/read/experience.)

## 3. Every element earns its place
A container, badge, shadow, icon, gradient or animation stays only if it adds information,
affordance, grouping or a deliberate part of the identity. Ask: *what does this communicate or
enable?* If the answer is "it looked empty without it", the real problem is missing content or weak
hierarchy, so fix that instead.

## 4. Hierarchy before decoration
Get reading order right with scale, weight, position, proximity and contrast before adding any
surface treatment. The squint test (blur the screen until the text is unreadable) should still
show the primary element, the secondary element and the groups in order.

## 5. Real content, real states
Design with real copy, real data lengths and real assets. A screen isn't finished until its loading,
empty, error, disabled and success states exist where they can happen. A good-looking mock
with buttons that do nothing is unfinished work. Never invent testimonials, customers or metrics.

## 6. One coherent system, not one universal system
Each project gets its own tokens and component rules, applied consistently. Consistency within a
project is a virtue. Sameness across projects is a failure.

## 7. Refinement preserves; redesign replaces
Polishing keeps the existing identity, behaviour and copy. A redesign may replace the look but
keeps the product's truth, content, function and brand commitments. Never quietly turn a polish
into a redesign, and never use polish on a look the user has asked to replace.

## 8. Verify by looking
Good CSS doesn't automatically mean good design. Judge the rendered result at the sizes it ships
at, compare it with the direction, fix and look again. Report exactly what was checked.

## 9. Variety comes from decisions, not randomness
Two projects differ because their products differ. Within a page, a section changes form only when
its content or priority changes. Repetition that helps recognition is fine. Repetition that comes
from a framework default is not.
